# Visual direction decisions

**Start here.** Write one sentence for what the visitor needs to do, what they should feel, and which real material can make this page distinct. Pick a visual idea that helps those three things meet.

## Choose how the page should behave

| If the brief needs | A direction worth trying | Main tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anticipation and sense of place | A poster, brochure or exhibition scene built around genuine objects, imagery or dates. Give the arrival decision a separate, plainly legible area. | Atmosphere can push the programme or booking details away. Compare a shorter mobile sequence. |
| Trust in an unfamiliar service | A task-led entry with clear verbs, prerequisites and hierarchy. Put material proof close to the claim it supports. | An overly anonymous visual system can hide who the service is for. Use specific words and project identity. |
| Comparison or repetitive work | A product-led layout with consistent rows, column alignment, helpful defaults and visible state. | Tight density can make scanning and touch harder. Adjust by viewport and test real long rows. |
| Recognition and desire | Product-led art direction or strong photography, supported by clear variants, price and a next step. | A campaign can compete with buying details. Move from mood to facts at the choice point. |
| Curiosity, surprise or play | Make one object, miniature experiment, surprising illustration or pointed line of copy carry the idea. Repeat the underlying principle once in a useful state. | A joke can be mistaken for a rule. Keep dates, names, availability, errors and recovery tied to the supplied facts. |
| Sustained reading | An editorial pace with consistent measure, meaningful image breaks, and a clear route through long material. | Oversized headings and repeated display typography slow scanning. Use scale where the narrative changes. |

Choose a posture that follows the user and material. Minimal can mean low visual effort in a transaction or carefully placed visual emphasis in a portfolio. A dark page can suggest a gallery at night, but so can a light programme laid out like an exhibition print. Start from different concepts when the default feels obvious. Try a quiet useful direction and one genuinely different idea at sketch scale when both plausibly fit. Give each a distinct opening composition, focal point and interaction path. Then keep the one that serves the brief under real mobile constraints.

## Make a visible decision

Describe what the reference changes in the rendered page, such as moving the price beside the decision, using a specimen cabinet instead of a logistics hero, or changing an empty state to a one-tap recovery. Borrow the decision and rebuild it around the project. In the Odd Hours study, reading two relevant cases changed the tone of the introduction. The distinctive composition came from turning the event's own objects into the hero visual. Check that both the story and the task survived the transfer.

Motion can show sequence, preserve orientation, or explain a change. Choose a trigger, an end state and an equivalent still presentation; reserve ambient motion for contexts that benefit from it. For a playful site, let visitors finish the task even if they never notice the joke.

If a project has an established identity, use those decisions as a starting system. On a scoped polish, change the implicated role only.
